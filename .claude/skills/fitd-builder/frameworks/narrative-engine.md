# Narrative Engine - Hook + Story for a FITD offer

Self-contained distillation of Russell Brunson's *Expert Secrets* (new opportunity,
big domino, epiphany bridge, false beliefs / 3 secrets, irresistible offer), tuned
for **foot-in-the-door offers an Extendly agency partner sells to a local business
niche**. This is everything the skill needs; it references no external files.

## Who the narrative is for

The **attraction hook** and **story** are aimed at the **local business owner** in
the chosen niche - the agency's prospect / end buyer. The goal: make that owner stop,
recognize their exact problem, and want the new vehicle. The agency owner deploys
this narrative (ad, VSL, landing page) to attract that niche.

> **Terminology guard.** Keep two "hooks" distinct:
> - **Attraction hook** (this framework): the ad/headline/opening line that makes the
>   niche stop and say "that's me." Top of funnel.
> - **Call hook** (OfferIQ pillar 2): the specific personalized result the lead magnet
>   hands the agency owner to run the sales call. Mid funnel.
> Never blur them in the output.

## 1. New opportunity, not improvement

Sell an **entirely new vehicle**, not a better version of what the niche already
tries. Improvement offers trigger resistance (past failure, ego, commodity pricing);
new opportunities activate desire and hope because the prospect never has to admit
prior failure.

**Status is the #1 driver.** Every buying decision answers "will this raise or lower
my status?" New opportunities win: no admission of failure + a fresh dream + the edge
of getting in early.

- **Who/What statement** (write this first):
  `"I help [WHO: the specific niche] [WHAT: the new result/vehicle]."`
- **Opportunity Switch Headline** (the core attraction-hook formula):
  `"How to [result they desire most] without [the thing they fear / sacrifice most]."`
  - e.g. "How to fill next month's chairs without spending another dollar on ads."
- **The 5 curiosity angles** (use to spin hook variants):
  1. **Little-known, big difference** - "There's a little-known [X] almost no [niche]
     is using. When you do, [result]."
  2. **Well known, little understood** - "Everyone in [niche] talks about [X]. What
     almost no one gets is [the real mechanism]."
  3. **This changes everything** - "Still doing [old approach]? A new [development]
     just changed the game for [niche]."
  4. **Crystal ball** - "[Old approach] isn't enough anymore. [New vehicle] is where
     [niche] is heading; get in before everyone else."
  5. **Back to fundamentals** - "[Niche] has overcomplicated this. Back to basics:
     just [simple steps]."

For a FITD offer the "new opportunity" is usually the **mechanism** behind the menu
offer reframed for the niche (e.g. Speed-to-Lead → "an always-on AI front desk for
[niche]"), not a brand-new product.

## 2. The Big Domino (the one belief)

Find the **single belief** that, once held, makes every other objection irrelevant.
Each extra belief you ask a prospect to hold roughly halves conversion, so aim at one.

**Big Domino statement** (fill for the niche + offer):
```
If I can make [the niche] believe that [new opportunity / mechanism] is the key to
[what they desire most] and is only attainable through [this offer / vehicle], then
all other objections become irrelevant and they have to take the offer.
```

**Logic spine (modus ponens):** If [new opportunity] is the key to [desired outcome],
and it is only available through [this vehicle], therefore [the niche needs this offer].

**Order matters: emotion first, logic second.** People buy emotionally (via story)
then justify logically (via features/proof). Leading with mechanics before the
emotional epiphany kills the sale. Sequence: story → emotional buy-in → logic as
confirmation.

- **"Kinda like" bridge** - translate any unfamiliar term into something the niche
  already knows: `"[Unfamiliar thing]. It's kinda like [familiar analogy]."` One
  unbridged unknown word ends attention for everything after it.
- **Third-grade clarity** - short words, short sentences. Complexity loses people.

## 3. The Epiphany Bridge (the story)

A persuasive story runs two journeys at once: the visible **Journey of Achievement**
(external goal) and the hidden **Journey of Transformation** (internal belief shift).
The transformation is what makes it stick. For a FITD offer the hero is **a relatable
owner in the niche** (or the agency owner's own origin) who discovered the new vehicle.

**Plot seed:** `"Once there was [character], who wanted [desire]. But [conflict]."`

**The 8-part script** (write each beat; fill-ins shown):

1. **Backstory** - "Before [new opportunity], I was [situation that mirrors where the
   niche owner is now]. I wanted [desire] but struggled with [external challenge]."
   → makes the listener think *that's me*.
2. **Desires (name both layers)** -
   - External: "I wanted to [visible goal] but couldn't [obstacle]."
   - Internal: "What I didn't say out loud was I felt [vulnerable emotion] because
     [root cause - usually love or status]."
3. **The wall** - "I was using [old approach] and it seemed fine, until [event]. That's
   when I realized [old approach] would never get me there. I felt [specific emotion]."
   The wall is the failure of the OLD vehicle, not a random new problem.
4. **The epiphany** - "Then [person / event / moment] showed me [new way of seeing it].
   I realized [new belief], which led me to [new opportunity / vehicle]."
5. **The plan** - "Armed with that, my plan was to [step], then [step], then [step]."
6. **The conflict** - point of no return ("I HAD to, no turning back") + a major
   setback ("it looked over, then I noticed [glimmer of hope]").
7. **The achievement** - "After everything, [concrete result]."
8. **The transformation** - "More than [external result], what really changed was
   [internal shift]. I used to believe [old belief]; now I know [new belief]." → this
   is where the listener has their own epiphany.

**Feeling injection** - don't summarize emotions, stage them: where you were, who
else was there, the physical sensation, the metaphor, then the label. Excavate the
internal struggle with "why?" five times; it usually bottoms out at love or status.

The **origin story** is the epiphany bridge that proves *why you believe the Big
Domino*. Validation: after hearing it, does the niche owner now believe the Big Domino
statement?

## 4. False beliefs → the 3 Secrets

Beliefs form as **experience → story → belief → action**. You can't out-logic a false
belief; you replace it with a new experience delivered as a story. Sort every
objection the niche holds into three buckets, and pair each with a short epiphany
bridge that shatters it.

| # | Category | The objection sounds like | For a FITD offer / niche |
|---|----------|---------------------------|--------------------------|
| 1 | **Vehicle** | "This might work, but not for MY kind of business / niche." | "AI/reviews/etc. won't work for [niche] specifically." |
| 2 | **Internal** | "I believe in it, but I can't pull it off." | "I'm not techy / too busy / my staff won't use it." |
| 3 | **External** | "I could, but outside forces stop me." | "No time, no budget, my customers won't talk to a bot, my market's different." |

**Reframe each false belief as a curiosity "Secret":**
```
Secret #[N]: How to [achieve desired result] [specific mechanism/angle]
            [without / even if] [the feared obstacle this secret addresses].
```
- Secret 1 (vehicle): proves the vehicle works *for this niche*.
- Secret 2 (internal): removes the skill/effort gap (done-for-you, templated, "we set it up").
- Secret 3 (external): dissolves the outside-force excuse (time, money, customer reaction).

**Knockdown sequence:** opportunity-switch headline → Big Domino statement → origin
epiphany story (Domino starts to fall) → Secret 1 → Secret 2 → Secret 3 (each support
knocked out) → Domino falls → offer.

For FITD work, surface the niche's real false beliefs from the research step (their
words, their past failures), not generic ones.

## 5. Irresistible offer (light, for FITD)

A full *Expert Secrets* stack (10x value, 6-element stack) is for flagship offers. A
FITD offer is the **foot**, not the door - keep the stack lean and let OfferIQ's
pillars carry the mechanics. Still useful:

- **Value framing:** state value as *the result is worth* to the niche owner, not the
  hours of work ("answering every lead instantly is worth a full-time front-desk
  salary").
- **Belief-breaker assets map to the 3 secrets:** a vehicle proof (case/example from
  the niche), an internal reliever (we do the setup), an external reliever (live in
  ~72h, no new headcount).
- The **ascension** (OfferIQ pillar 7) is where the real stack lives later; the FITD
  offer only needs to be an easy, obvious yes.
